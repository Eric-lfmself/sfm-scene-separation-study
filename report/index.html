Two Silent Failures in SfM Matching

A Structure-from-Motion matching pipeline — DINOv2 shortlisting → ALIKED → LightGlue → COLMAP — ported to current libraries and measured against laser-scanned ground truth. It reconstructs single scenes to millimetres, quietly welds two of them together, and then defeats the obvious fix.

Setup

What is being reproduced, and against what

The evaluation set is ETH3D high-resolution multi-view, chosen for one property above all: every image carries a ground-truth camera pose derived from laser-scan registration, in COLMAP format. That makes it possible to score pose accuracy directly rather than only counting how many images registered.

All pipeline hyper-parameters are the original configuration, unchanged: ALIKED at 4600 keypoints / 1024 px / threshold 0.08, DINOv2-base MAC descriptors with sim_th=0.3 and min_pairs=58, LightGlue with min_matches=20, and the mapper at min_model_size=3, max_num_models=25.

Two metrics, both standard

AUC@5/10/20° — the convention across the feature-matching literature. Per image pair, error is max(rotation angular error, translation angular error), and AUC is the normalised area under the cumulative error curve at each threshold. That is the same error definition LightGlue reports AUC@5/10/20° against. A pair that is unregistered — or split across two reconstructions, where relative pose is meaningless — scores 180°.

Sim(3)-aligned absolute error — the Structure-from-Motion convention. A reconstruction is defined only up to a similarity transform, so it is first aligned to ground truth with a Sim(3); reported as median camera-centre and median absolute rotation error. ETH3D ground truth is metrically scaled from laser scans, so positions are real metres.

Result · mixed scenes

Three scenes in one folder: two of them merge

The hard case for this pipeline is that images from unrelated scenes arrive in one bucket and it has to separate them. Setting that up here means shuffling courtyard, terrace and pipes into a single 75-image directory.

Every image still registers. But COLMAP returns two reconstructions, not three:

 Cluster purity 0.693. courtyard degrades from 516 mm standalone to 809 mm and AUC@5° 0.744 → 0.646, while terrace (12 mm) and pipes (5 mm) are barely touched: the merge damages the larger, weaker scene and leaves its partner's numbers intact. Registration counts alone would show nothing.

The merge is not a mapper quirk. It is fed by matches: 296 of the kept image pairs are courtyard↔terrace, against 661 within-courtyard and 210 within-terrace. Both scenes sit on the ETH Zürich campus and share facade, railing and paving texture, so ALIKED and LightGlue find real, locally-consistent correspondences between them. 147 of those cross-scene pairs then survive COLMAP's geometric verification.

Fix · attempt

One filter, applied after verification

Dropping every two-view geometry with fewer than 100 inliers — 388 of 1040 — before handing the database to the mapper, with matching otherwise untouched. On this dataset every metric moves the right way, including the ones that had nothing to do with clustering:

Cluster purity 0.693→1.000 3 clusters, one per scene, exactly. Still 75/75 registered.
courtyard position 809 mm→378 mm Beats its own 516 mm standalone run. AUC@5° 0.646 → 0.779.
courtyard rotation 4.38°→1.78° terrace and pipes unchanged, as they were already correct.
Mapping time 449 s→172 s 2.6× faster — the mapper stops chasing weak edges.

A clean result, and the obvious thing to recommend. So the next experiment was built to break it.

Falsification

The same filter, on the opposite problem

A weak link between two images has two possible causes, and the filter cannot tell them apart. It might be a false link between different places that look alike — the courtyard/terrace case. Or it might be a true link between two captures of the same place under different conditions: a revisit.

ETH3D's relief and relief_2 are exactly that — 31 + 31 images of the same interior, photographed in two sessions. DINOv2 cannot separate them at all (cross-session descriptor distance 0.305 against 0.308 and 0.295 within), because there is nothing to separate. Here the correct output is one cluster, and any split is the failure.

 Correct answer is 1 cluster; the baseline already fails. The two clusters are not the two sessions — one holds 13 relief + 11 relief_2, the other 18 + 20. Cross-session linking worked; the reconstruction fragmented spatially instead.

The filtered relief_2 reconstruction did not merely get less accurate — it collapsed. After Sim(3) alignment every one of its 20 cameras points more than 178° from its true orientation, while the camera positions still fit to within a metre. The model looks plausible until you check where the cameras are pointing. The alignment is a proper rotation, so this is the reconstruction and not the comparison.

The filter deleted 431 of 780 verified geometries to get there. It had to: true cross-session links are weak, and their inlier counts sit in the same range as the false cross-scene links the filter was designed to remove.

What this actually establishes

Inlier count alone cannot distinguish "a different place that looks similar" from "the same place, seen again under different conditions". A threshold high enough to remove all 150 false cross-scene links (84) also removes 80% of the 370 true cross-session links. On a survey where flight strips overlap and lighting changes between passes, that is the difference between one reconstruction and several.

What this pins down

The merge is not caused by mixing. It needs two distinct places that genuinely look alike, producing correspondences strong enough to survive RANSAC. An industrial building and a rubble field do not, so verification cleans up unaided and the mapper never sees a reason to join them. That also says where the risk lives in practice: repeated architecture, not scene count.

The first aerial attempt measured nothing, and it looked like a result

The obvious slice to take is the benchmark's own validation split. It produced 3 reconstructions from 20 images, the largest holding 5 — which reads like a clustering failure and is not one. That split samples roughly every 97th frame of a ~1900-image flight, so consecutive images barely overlap: only 35% of pairs matched at all. A benchmark split built for one task (novel-view synthesis) can be actively wrong for another, and the symptom is indistinguishable from the failure you are hunting. Streaming consecutive frames out of the 11 GB archives fixed it.

No ground-truth poses were obtainable for the aerial frames — the pose metadata sits at the tail of an 11 GB archive the host stopped serving mid-study — so these rows report registration and clustering only, both of which are metric-independent.

Retrieval

The parameter that only starts working at 250 images

min_pairs=58 forces each image to keep its 58 nearest DINOv2 neighbours. Across every run in this study, that floor — not the sim_th similarity test — is what decides the shortlist, and below roughly 250 images it leaves almost nothing pruned:

Conclusion

What survives both experiments

  • The port is correct: full registration on three single ETH3D scenes, two of them within 16 mm of a laser scan, and 120/120 in one cluster on a real UAV site.
  • The pipeline silently merges distinct scenes that share visual style, and silently fragments a single scene captured twice. Both failures are invisible without ground truth.
  • A global post-verification inlier threshold fixes the first failure and worsens the second. It is a dataset-specific patch, not a fix.
  • What a real fix needs is a signal the inlier count does not carry — spatial priors (GPS/IMU, which UAV survey data has and neither of these benchmarks exposes), or reasoning over the match graph's structure rather than over edge weights one at a time.
  • Twice the expensive failure was a measurement artefact rather than a pipeline defect: a benchmark split with no overlap, and a "repeated structure" scene pair that turned out to be the same place photographed twice. Checking what the data actually contains came before every real finding here.
Porting

What actually broke in three years

The notebook pinned cp310 wheels that no longer resolve on Colab's Python 3.13. Current PyPI versions install cleanly, but four API changes stop the code:

  1. 1

    COLMAP grew a rig/frame schema

    pycolmap 4.x requires every image to belong to a frame and every frame to a rig, so the classic h5_to_db.py and database.py SQLite writers produce an obsolete schema. Replaced with one trivial rig + frame per image via the modern Database API.

  2. 2

    Database() has no constructor

    Construction moved to the factory pycolmap.Database.open(path).

  3. 3

    Image.cam_from_world is now a method

    It is derived from the image's frame rather than stored, so reading it as an attribute yields a bound method and fails silently downstream.

  4. 4

    kornia.io.load_image is broken against current kornia_rs

    It calls read_image_jpegturbo, renamed to read_image_jpeg. Swapped for a cv2 loader with identical output contract — (1,3,H,W) float32 RGB in [0,1]. (Separately, import kornia no longer pulls in kornia.utils.)

Limits

What this does not establish

  • Five ETH3D scenes and two UAV sites, at most 251 images per run. Each failure was observed on one specific scene pair; neither is established as general.
  • Single run per configuration. COLMAP's incremental mapper is seed-dependent, so small AUC differences are not necessarily real; the large ones — a 178° rotation collapse, a purity change from 0.693 to 1.000 — are well outside that noise.
  • The aerial evidence is single-site only so far. The overlap between true and false weak links is demonstrated on ground-level imagery; the 251-frame mixed UAV run has not finished mapping.
  • Mill 19 poses are a PixSfM reconstruction, not independent survey ground truth, so aerial pose agreement measures consistency with a careful reference rather than absolute accuracy.
